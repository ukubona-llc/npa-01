04 — How It Works

Five dimensions.
One decision.

▲ State heat map

Location — where exactly?
Every district in Uganda has a fixed coordinate — longitude and latitude. The twin anchors every data point to a real place on the ground. Nothing floats as a national average. A figure like "24.2% mental health prevalence" is disaggregated: Gulu is not Kampala is not Karamoja. The coordinate is the foundation.
State — how bad is it here?
The ROYGBIV colour encodes severity at a glance. Red districts demand immediate attention. Blue districts are the model to replicate. The gradient between them is a policy triage queue. Before any meeting, the board can see which ten districts need discussion — without reading a single table.
Focus — which question are we asking?
The dropdown reframes the map without changing the underlying data. Switch from teenage pregnancy to mental health burden to workforce gaps — same 135 districts, different altitude, different priority queue. The board sets the agenda; the twin responds. This is the part that replaces a six-week consultant report with a thirty-second interaction.
Flow — how does data get here?
The pipeline is what makes the twin live, not static. District → NPA → Ukubona engine → intervention target → district update. When a district officer enters new figures, the heat map updates. When an intervention is deployed, the outcome feeds back. There is no quarterly report delay — the system reflects reality as it changes.
Insight — what do we do?
The twin does not make decisions. It makes decisions possible. A red district in the northwest with a workforce gap of 55% and a teenage pregnancy rate twice the national average is not a statistic — it is a specific instruction: deploy here, resource this, measure that. The insight is earned by running the full pipeline, not assumed from a national headline.

The pipeline, in full:

District data → NPA policy frame → Ukubona engine → Intervention target → Outcome update → District recalibrates

05 — The Finance Argument

The cost of not knowing.

This is not a technology purchase. It is an economic argument. Uganda already has the data. The question is whether that data reaches decision-makers in time to be useful.

Cost of inaction — mental health alone
US$4.5B
Projected annual economic losses by 2040 under business-as-usual. SUPRE 2025 figures. Productivity loss dominates — not treatment costs. This number grows faster than GDP.
The structural problem
71%
Rise in facility-reported mental health cases 2021–2024 — yet fewer than 1 in 10 people in need receive care. The cases are visible. The response is not matching.
The workforce gap
55%
Of required district-level mental health posts are unfilled. 5,371 posts missing. 96% of the gap is at community level — HC III, HC IV, General Hospital — not at national referral. The absence is precisely where early detection must happen.
What the twin changes
Latency → zero
The delay between reality on the ground and a decision at the centre is where the cost accumulates. The twin collapses that delay. No quarterly report cycle. No data lost in translation. No district invisible because they didn't submit on time.
The data already exists.
The workforce gaps are already counted.
The question is: how long does it take for that information to change what a district officer does on Monday morning? — The core planning argument

06 — What This Enables

One engine.
Every territory.

The same architecture that maps Uganda's districts maps a kingdom, a ministry, a municipality. The engine does not change — only the coordinate frame and the indicators.

The report tells you what.
The twin tells you where, when, and how much.

SUPRE 2025 is a national-level picture. Its findings are correct and compelling. But a national average cannot tell a district health officer in Gulu what to do on Monday morning. The Ukubona digital twin compresses the national picture to district-level signals — specific, updatable, and directly linked from data collection through to outcome measurement.

What it shows
146 districts · ROYGBIV burden map · reweights instantly by policymaker priority
What it replaces
Static reports that age on printing · national averages that hide district variation · planning without a feedback loop
N = 2 or 3 variables
The twin does not require a PhD to read. Two or three variables surface the decision. Complexity lives in the engine; the output is a map and a direction.
The feedback loop
Interventions are measured. The twin recalibrates. The system learns. That is the point of a digital twin — it is not a snapshot, it is a living instrument.
Scalable to any territory
National · Kingdom · Ministry · District · Sub-county — same engine, different coordinate frame, different indicators.
Data sovereignty
Whoever holds the API keys controls the data. In this model, the keys stay with Government. The compute is rented; the data is sovereign.
